    How to plan a surf trip with Surflist

    The useful question is not "which surf school is best". It is "which town can I reach, with a beach that works for me, and who actually operates there". Surflist is organised that way: country, then region, then town.

    Pick a destination you can actually get to

    A surf trip falls apart on logistics more often than on waves. Flights, trains, a car, and how far the beach is from the bed matter as much as the forecast. Start on the [destinations](/) list and open a country you can reach — England, Portugal, Morocco, Indonesia and the others we cover — then drill into a region and a town.

    Each country page is a set of regions, each region a set of towns. That is the map. You are not browsing a random grid of businesses; you are choosing a place, then seeing who is on the ground.

    Match the town to your level and your week

    Beginner weeks want a town with several schools, a sandy beach break, and somewhere to hire a board if the lesson finishes and the sea is still friendly. Improver weeks can lean on towns with more than one beach, so you can move when the wind turns onshore. Advanced trips are usually about a specific break — and those towns still need a backup beach for the days it is out of control.

    Read the town hub before you book anything. Taghazout, Ericeira and Canggu are examples of places where the directory already has enough listings to show a proper hub: schools, and often shops or stays too.

    Build the trip in this order

    1. Town — can you get there, and is there a beach for your level?
    2. Stay — walking distance to the beach you will actually use.
    3. School or hire — a lesson for the first days, hire once you are comfortable.
    4. Shop and repair — wax, a leash, a ding fix. You only care until you need them.

    That order keeps you from booking a famous school in a town you cannot stay in, or a camp that sits on the wrong beach for the swell that week.

    Schools, shops, stays and services sit in the same town

    Surflist is four categories on purpose. A surf school gets you in the water. A surf shop is where you replace the leash you snapped. A stay is a camp, hostel or guesthouse that understands wet kit. Surf services are the ding repairs and the other jobs around the trip.

    Not every town has all four. That is honest. A town with two schools and no shop still gets a hub; a town with one listing does not, until a second real business is confirmed. Don't invent a second stop to make the map look fuller — pick a neighbouring town that is actually listed.

    Use the listing page, then book on the school's site

    Every surf school has a page on Surflist. That page is for choosing, not for taking payment. When you are ready, the visit and booking links go to the business. Shops, stays and services still link out until they have the same treatment, unless they are a paid verified listing.

    Forecasts, tide times and "is it good tomorrow" are not what this directory is for. Use a forecast you trust, then use Surflist to see who is in that town when the sea is worth it.

    A short packing reality check

    You need a way to get wet kit home or a way to dry it. You need something to eat within walking distance of the beach if the session runs long. You need a plan if the wind is wrong for three days — another beach, a shop day, or a lesson moved. The town hub is where those alternatives live, because the other schools and stays in the same place are the backup, not a different country.

    FAQs

    Should I book the school before the stay?

    Book the place you will sleep first if peak season is tight, then the school. A lesson you can walk to beats a cheaper lesson you have to drive to in a wet wetsuit.

    What if a town has no hub page?

    Town hubs only appear once there are at least two listings. A single school can still have its own page under surf schools. The missing hub means the town is thin in the directory, not that the place has no surf.
