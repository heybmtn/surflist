The Ultimate Guide to Surfing in Newquay (2026): Best Seasons, Surf Spots & Water Temperatures

We've been coming back to Newquay for years, and it still feels like the beating heart of British surfing. On a good autumn morning, with clean lines rolling into Fistral and the car park slowly filling up, there's nowhere we'd rather paddle out. This guide is everything we've learned from those trips. We'll cover the best seasons, the water temperatures month by month, the wetsuit you'll want, the best surf spots, where beginners should start, and what to pack. Newquay isn't the warmest place to surf, and it isn't the emptiest. But few towns give you this many waves in one small stretch of coast — and that's what makes it special.

More than ten beaches in one small town

Newquay sits on the north coast of Cornwall, and its greatest trick is choice. There are more than ten sandy beaches within a few miles of the town centre. They all face slightly different directions. So when the wind is wrong at one beach, it's often right at another. If Fistral is blown out, you can drive ten minutes and find something cleaner at Watergate Bay or tucked in at Towan. That variety is the whole reason Newquay works so well. You rarely get skunked here.

Waves all year round

Newquay picks up surf in every season. Winter brings the biggest, most powerful swells. Spring is cooler and quieter, with waves easing down. Summer is the gentlest time, with smaller, friendlier waves and the warmest water. Autumn is the sweet spot, when clean Atlantic swells return but the water is still mild. There's almost always something to ride.

The swell comes straight off the Atlantic

The north Cornwall coast faces the open Atlantic. That's why the surf is so reliable. Low-pressure storms track across the ocean and send swell towards the coast, and Newquay's beaches sit right in the firing line. Winter storms make the biggest waves. In summer the swells are smaller and less frequent, but they still come. Because the coast is so exposed, you'll get surf on most days of the year.

Suits every skill level

This is one of the few places where a total beginner and a seasoned surfer can both have a great day, a short walk apart. Fistral has punchy, powerful peaks for confident surfers. Towan and the town beaches offer soft, slow waves for first-timers. Crantock and Watergate Bay are great for longboarders who like a mellow, rolling wave. Whatever your level, there's usually something nearby that suits you. Browse surf schools in Newquay if you want a lesson.

Climate and life between sessions

Cornwall is mild for the UK, thanks to the Gulf Stream. It rarely freezes in winter, and summers are warm rather than hot. Between surfs there's plenty to do. You can walk the South West Coast Path along the cliffs, eat a pasty on the harbour wall, or dry off in one of the town's many cafés and pubs. It's an easy place to fill a day out of the water.

A proper surf town

Newquay has surfed since the 1960s, and it shows. Surf schools, board shops and hire shacks are everywhere. Clubs like Newquay Boardriders have been running for decades. The town can feel busy and lively in summer, especially with stag and hen groups about, but the surf community underneath it all is warm and welcoming. Ask a local where's working on the day and most will happily point you in the right direction.

Getting there

Newquay is easier to reach than most of Cornwall. Cornwall Airport Newquay sits about 6 kilometres (4 miles) from the town, roughly a ten-minute drive, with flights from London, Manchester and other UK cities. If you're driving, the A30 brings you down through the county from the M5 at Exeter. There's also a branch train line into Newquay station. Once you're in town, the main beaches are close together, and you can reach Fistral, Towan and Watergate Bay in minutes.

Surfing Newquay by season

There's no single best time to surf Newquay. It depends on what you want. Beginners and families do best in the warm, gentle days of summer. Surfers chasing size and power come in winter. If you want the best all-round mix — decent swell, mild water and thinner crowds — autumn is hard to beat. Here's how each season breaks down.

🌸 Spring (March–May)

Best for: improvers who want quiet line-ups and don't mind cold water.

Spring is a quiet, in-between season. The huge winter swells start to ease, so the waves become more manageable. The crowds haven't arrived yet, so line-ups are calm. There's space to practise and time to catch waves.

The catch is the cold. March has the coldest water of the whole year, colder even than midwinter, because the sea takes months to warm up after winter. You'll still need a thick wetsuit and probably boots. But if you can handle the chill, spring gives you clean waves and plenty of room.

Average conditions

  • Water temperature: 9–12°C (48–54°F)
  • Air temperature: 9–13°C (48–55°F)
  • Recommended wetsuit: 5/4mm early, dropping to 4/3mm by May, with boots

Best regions

  • Fistral
  • Watergate Bay

☀️ Summer (June–August)

Best for: beginners, families and first-timers who want warm water and gentle waves.

Summer is the friendliest time to learn. The water is at its warmest. The waves are usually smaller and softer, which is exactly what you want when you're starting out. The surf schools are in full swing, and the whole town has a holiday buzz.

The trade-off is crowds. Newquay is a major summer resort, so the beaches and the water fill up, especially in the school holidays. Waves can also go flat for days at a time when there's no swell. And in early August the town gets very busy for Boardmasters, the big surf-and-music festival.

Boardmasters runs from Wednesday 5 to Sunday 9 August 2026. The surf competition and beach village at Fistral are free to watch, while the music site at Watergate Bay needs a ticket. It's a brilliant few days, but book accommodation early and expect the town to be packed.

Average conditions

  • Water temperature: 14–17°C (57–63°F)
  • Air temperature: 16–20°C (61–68°F)
  • Recommended wetsuit: 3/2mm, or a shorty on the warmest days

Best regions

  • Towan and the town beaches
  • Crantock

🍂 Autumn (September–November)

Best for: all levels — the best all-round season.

Autumn is our favourite time to surf Newquay. The reason is simple. The sea is still mild from the summer, but the clean Atlantic swells are starting to return. So you get quality waves without the deep cold of winter.

The summer crowds thin out once the schools go back, so line-ups open up again. Early autumn still works well for beginners on the gentler beaches. By November the waves get bigger and the water starts to chill, so you'll want a warmer suit and boots.

If we could only pick one season, this would be it.

Average conditions

  • Water temperature: 13–16°C (55–61°F)
  • Air temperature: 12–17°C (54–63°F)
  • Recommended wetsuit: 3/2mm early, moving to 4/3mm and boots by November

Best regions

  • Fistral
  • Watergate Bay
  • Crantock

❄️ Winter (December–February)

Best for: experienced surfers chasing size and power.

Winter is when Newquay shows its teeth. The Atlantic storms are at their strongest, so this is the season for the biggest, most powerful surf. Fistral can turn on properly, and the coast comes alive when a clean swell lines up with light offshore winds.

This is not the time for beginners. The water is cold, the waves are heavy, and rip currents are stronger. You'll need a thick winter wetsuit plus boots, gloves and a hood to stay in for any length of time. But the crowds are at their lowest, and the payoff on the right day is huge.

Average conditions

  • Water temperature: 9–11°C (48–52°F)
  • Air temperature: 8–11°C (46–52°F)
  • Recommended wetsuit: 5/4mm with boots, gloves and hood

Best regions

  • Fistral
  • Watergate Bay

Newquay's water temperatures throughout the year

Newquay's sea temperature is driven by the Atlantic. The ocean is slow to heat up and slow to cool down. That's why the coldest water comes in late winter and early spring — around March — not in December. And the warmest water comes in late summer, in August, after months of sun. Is the water cold?

Yes, but less than you might fear. Cornwall has the mildest sea in the UK, thanks to the Gulf Stream. In winter the water sits around 9–11°C (48–52°F), which is a few degrees warmer than the north of England or Scotland. In late summer it reaches about 16–17°C (61–63°F). That's still cool compared with somewhere like Portugal or the Canary Islands. But a good wetsuit changes everything. In summer a 3/2mm is plenty. In winter, a 5/4mm with boots, gloves and a hood keeps you warm enough to enjoy it.

Small differences from beach to beach

The water temperature is much the same across Newquay's beaches — they all sit on the same open coast. What changes is how sheltered they feel. Beaches like Crantock and Towan are more tucked in, so they feel warmer and calmer on a windy day. Big, open Fistral and Watergate Bay catch more swell and more wind, which can make them feel colder even when the sea is the same. It's the wind chill, more than the water, that you'll notice.

The best surf spots in Newquay

The joy of Newquay is how much you can pack into a short drive. You've got a world-class beach break, a string of sheltered town beaches, a huge open bay just up the road, and a mellow estuary beach to the south. Here's how we'd think about each one.

Fistral Beach

Fistral is the star, and rightly so. Many people call it the home of British surfing. It faces west and catches Atlantic swell almost all year round, which makes it the most reliable beach in the area. The waves here are powerful and punchy, and on a big clean day the barrels can be world-class. It hosts the surf side of Boardmasters every August.

The beach splits into North and South Fistral. North Fistral is the heavier end and draws stronger surfers. If you're less experienced, head to the middle or the southern end, where the waves are usually softer. Fistral gets busy, so expect a full car park and a busy line-up on the best days.

Best for:

  • Intermediate and advanced surfers
  • Anyone chasing power and the odd barrel
  • Surfers who want the most reliable waves in Newquay

Who should visit Fistral?

  • Confident surfers who want quality beach-break waves
  • Anyone happy to share a busy line-up
  • Surfers who want one beach that almost always has something

The town beaches: Towan, Great Western, Tolcarne and Lusty Glaze

Just around the headland from Fistral sit Newquay's town beaches. Towan, Great Western, Tolcarne and Lusty Glaze all face roughly north-west and share Newquay Bay. Because they're more sheltered than Fistral, they're often cleaner when the wind is up, and the waves are usually gentler. That makes this the natural home for beginners and surf schools. See surf schools in Newquay.

Towan Beach

Towan is the most central beach, right by the town, and it's the classic learner's spot. The harbour wall shelters it, so the waves are soft and slow. It's ideal for first lessons. One thing to know: in summer, surfing is restricted at certain times to keep swimmers safe, so check before you paddle out.

Best for:

  • Complete beginners
  • Families
  • First surf lessons

Great Western, Tolcarne and Lusty Glaze

These smaller bays sit next to Towan and offer more of the same sheltered, forgiving surf. They're quieter than Towan and good for improvers who want a calmer beach. Lusty Glaze is a privately owned beach but is open to the public.

Best for:

  • Beginners and improvers
  • Anyone wanting a calmer, less crowded beach

Who should visit the town beaches?

  • Beginners taking their first lessons
  • Families surfing together
  • Anyone who wants shelter when Fistral is windy

Watergate Bay

Watergate Bay is a huge sweep of sand just north of Newquay, stretching about 3 kilometres (2 miles). Its size is its magic. There's so much room that the crowds spread out, so it rarely feels busy even on a good day. It catches plenty of swell and works for all levels, with softer, more forgiving waves than Fistral. Longboarders and intermediates love it for clean, rolling walls. It also hosts national surfing events. See surf schools in Watergate Bay.

Best for:

  • All levels, from improver to advanced
  • Longboarders after long, clean waves
  • Anyone who wants space in the line-up

Who should visit Watergate Bay?

  • Surfers who dislike crowds
  • Longboarders and intermediates
  • Families wanting a big open beach with facilities

Crantock

Just south of Newquay, across the Gannel Estuary, sits Crantock. It's a calmer, prettier spot, backed by sand dunes. The estuary shapes the sandbanks here, which can make for mellow, playful waves that suit beginners and longboarders. Because it's a little harder to reach, Crantock stays quieter than Fistral. It's the beach we head to when we want a relaxed session away from the crowds.

Best for:

  • Beginners and longboarders
  • Intermediates on the right day
  • Anyone after a quieter, scenic surf

Who should visit Crantock?

  • Longboarders who like mellow, rolling waves
  • Surfers escaping the Fistral crowds
  • Anyone who likes a beautiful, dune-backed beach

Our personal favourite

If we had to choose, we'd split our time between Watergate Bay and Fistral. Fistral for the days when the swell lines up and we want quality waves with real punch. Watergate Bay when Fistral is too crowded, or when we just want space to spread out and surf without hassle. Fistral has the best waves, but Watergate has the room — and on a busy summer week, that room is worth a lot.

The best beginner surf spots in Newquay

Newquay is one of the best places in Britain to learn to surf. The reason is choice. Whatever the conditions, there's usually a sheltered beach with soft, slow waves and a surf school ready to help. Here are the spots we'd point a new surfer towards.

1. Towan Beach

Best for: first-ever lessons and nervous beginners.

Towan is the classic learner's beach. The harbour wall keeps it sheltered, so the waves stay gentle and easy to catch. It's central, easy to reach, and full of surf schools. Just remember the summer surfing restrictions during busy swimming hours. Browse Newquay surf schools.

Why we recommend it:

  • Sheltered, soft, slow waves
  • Right by the town, easy to reach
  • Plenty of surf schools on hand

2. Watergate Bay

Best for: beginners who want space to practise.

Watergate Bay is big and open, with room for everyone. The waves are forgiving, and there's so much sand that you won't feel crowded. Several surf schools run here for exactly that reason. It's a great step up once you've had your first few lessons. See Watergate Bay surf schools.

Why we recommend it:

  • Loads of space, even in summer
  • Soft, rolling, beginner-friendly waves
  • Good surf schools and facilities

3. Crantock Beach

Best for: beginners who want a quieter, calmer session.

Crantock is more sheltered and less crowded than the main beaches. The estuary sandbanks often make mellow, gentle waves that are kind to learners. It's a lovely place to practise away from the summer rush.

Why we recommend it:

  • Calm, mellow waves on the right day
  • Quieter than Fistral or Towan
  • Beautiful, dune-backed setting

What makes a good beginner surf beach?

When we point a new surfer somewhere, we look for a few simple things. A sandy bottom, so there's nothing hard to hit. Gentle, rolling waves that break slowly and give you time to stand up. Shelter from the wind, which keeps the water cleaner and calmer. Enough space so you're not fighting for waves. And a good surf school nearby, because a lesson or two will speed you up far more than going it alone. Newquay ticks all of these, which is why it's such a fine place to start.

Should beginners surf in summer?

Yes — summer is the easiest time to learn in Newquay. The water is at its warmest, and the waves are usually smaller and softer. The surf schools are all running, so it's easy to book a lesson. The one downside is crowds. The beaches and line-ups get busy, especially in the school holidays and around Boardmasters in early August. To get the best of it, surf early in the morning before the beaches fill up. Early autumn is another great option: the water is still mild, but the crowds have thinned out.

Newquay surf trip packing list

🏄 Surf essentials

Surfboard

If you're a beginner, a big soft-top or a longer foam board is your friend — they're stable and easy to catch waves on. Most people hire one for the first few trips, which saves lugging a board across the country. Improvers and confident surfers will want a board that suits the conditions: a shortboard or all-rounder for punchy Fistral days, and a longboard for mellow, rolling waves at Watergate Bay or Crantock.

Wetsuit

Match your suit to the season. In summer, a 3/2mm is enough. In spring and autumn, go for a 4/3mm. In winter, you'll want a 5/4mm. Whatever the time of year, the sea here is never warm, so don't turn up with just boardshorts.

Boots, gloves and hood

From late autumn through early spring, these make cold-water surfing bearable. Your hands, feet and head lose heat fastest, so covering them keeps you in the water far longer. Bring all three for a winter trip.

Surf wax

Bring cold-water or cool-water wax for most of the year. Only in high summer will you need warm-water wax. Grab a spare bar — it's cheap and easy to lose.

Changing poncho or towel

A changing robe or poncho is a game-changer in a windy car park, especially in winter. It keeps you warm and lets you get out of a wet wetsuit with some dignity.

Useful extras

  • Reusable water bottle
  • Dry bag for wet wetsuits
  • Waterproof phone pouch
  • Small first aid kit
  • Earplugs (surfer's ear)
  • Ding repair kit
  • Flip-flops / sandals
  • Snorkel & mask for flat days

The best Newquay trip isn't the one with the biggest waves. It's the one where you read the day and pick the right beach for it. When Fistral is wild, slip round to the town beaches. When the crowds arrive, drive up to Watergate or south to Crantock. Say yes to the dawn session, then warm up with a pasty on the harbour. Surf the beach that suits the conditions, not the one with the famous name — and you'll leave already planning the next trip.

FAQs

Is Newquay good for beginner surfers?

Yes — it's one of the best places to learn in Britain. It has several sheltered beaches with soft, slow waves and lots of surf schools. Towan, Watergate Bay and Crantock are all kind to beginners. Whatever the wind is doing, there's usually a beach that works.

What is the best time of year to surf in Newquay?

It depends on what you want. Beginners do best in summer, when the water is warm and the waves are gentle. Experienced surfers prefer autumn and winter, when the Atlantic swells are bigger and cleaner. Our all-round pick is September or October: mild water, good waves and fewer crowds.

Can you surf in Newquay all year round?

Yes. Newquay picks up swell in every season. Winter has the biggest waves, summer the smallest. With the right wetsuit, you can surf comfortably twelve months of the year.

Do I need a wetsuit in Newquay?

Always. The sea here is never truly warm. A 3/2mm suit is fine in summer. In spring and autumn, use a 4/3mm. In winter, wear a 5/4mm with boots, gloves and a hood.

Is Fistral or Watergate Bay better for surfing?

Both are excellent, but they suit different things. Fistral has more powerful, punchy waves and is the more famous break, but it gets crowded. Watergate Bay is bigger and more open, with softer waves and far more space. If you want quality and power, choose Fistral. If you want room and a mellow session, choose Watergate.

Is Crantock good for surfing?

Yes, especially for beginners and longboarders. It's more sheltered and quieter than the main beaches, and the estuary sandbanks often make mellow, playful waves. It's a great place for a relaxed surf away from the crowds.

Can you longboard in Newquay?

Absolutely. Watergate Bay and Crantock are ideal, with long, clean, rolling waves that suit a longboard. Fistral works too on smaller, gentler days. Newquay has a strong longboarding scene.

Is surfing in Newquay expensive?

It doesn't have to be. Board and wetsuit hire is affordable, and group lessons are good value. Watching the surf competition at Fistral during Boardmasters is free. Your biggest cost will be accommodation, which jumps in summer and around festival week. Book early and travel outside the school holidays to save money.

Is Newquay a good destination for a surf road trip?

It's a brilliant base for one. With more than ten beaches nearby, you can chase the best conditions each day. And it's an easy launchpad for the rest of the coast — Perranporth, Holywell Bay and Mawgan Porth are all a short drive away, with more waves and fewer crowds.
