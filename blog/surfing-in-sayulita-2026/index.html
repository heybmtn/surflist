The Ultimate Guide to Surfing in Sayulita (2026): Best Seasons, Surf Spots & Water Temperatures

We keep coming back to Sayulita. It's a small Pacific town in Nayarit, about an hour north of Puerto Vallarta, and it mixes easy beach-break waves with a lively, walkable centre that is hard to leave. We've had long, gentle sessions on the town beach at first light, and we've jumped in a car to chase cleaner peaks down the coast when the main break filled up. This guide covers everything we wish we'd known before our first trip — the best seasons, the surf spots by skill level, the water temperatures month by month, and what to pack. Whatever your level, and whatever the swell is doing, there is almost always a wave nearby that suits you.

A warm-water surf town on Banderas Bay

Sayulita sits on Mexico's Pacific coast in Nayarit, on the north side of Banderas Bay. It is a former fishing village that grew into one of the country's best-known small surf towns: cobbled streets, a packed main beach, and a line-up that works for first-timers as well as improvers.

The town beach is the heart of it. On a small day it is friendly and sandy. On a bigger south swell it steepens up and the take-off gets busier. When that happens, the coast nearby gives you options — San Pancho a few minutes north, and the Punta Mita beaches (including La Lancha) a short drive south.

Waves all year round

You can surf here every month of the year. Each season has its own feel. The dry months, roughly November to April, bring smaller, gentler waves and the busiest streets. The wet months, roughly May to October, bring larger south and south-west swell, hotter air, and fewer visitors in town.

That split is the key to planning a trip. Come in winter if you want to learn. Come in summer if you want more power. Come in November if you want a bit of both.

Where the swell comes from

Sayulita faces west into the open Pacific, so it sees swell from more than one direction.

From late spring through autumn, storms in the Southern Hemisphere and tropical systems in the eastern Pacific send south and south-west groundswell up this coast. That is when the town beach has more size, and when the points around Punta Mita really come alive.

From late autumn through early spring, north and north-west swell is more common. The waves are smaller and more playful, which is why winter is the classic learner season. Afternoon onshore wind is common in every season, so we surf early, when the water is usually cleanest.

Suits every skill level

This is the thing we love most about Sayulita. The town beach has soft, sandy waves that are great for beginners on small days. A little more swell, and it becomes a fun wall for intermediates. Down the coast, the Punta Mita spots add punch for surfers who want a step up. Browse surf schools in Sayulita if you want a lesson.

Longboarders are well looked after too. The mellow town-beach days are made for slow, stylish surfing.

Climate and lifestyle

The weather here is tropical and easy to live with. The dry season (November to April) is sunny, with warm days and cooler nights. The wet season (May to October) is hotter and more humid, with short, heavy afternoon rains and the odd tropical storm.

Air temperatures sit around 26–32°C (79–90°F) for much of the year. Even in January it rarely feels cold once the sun is up.

Between sessions there is plenty to do. The town is walkable, with taco stands, coffee shops and a busy plaza. You can paddle, hike the hills behind town, or take a boat trip on the bay. Mexico's Pacific coast has a lot of surf towns; Sayulita is the one that mixes waves with a proper village in a small space.

Surf culture

Sayulita lives and breathes surfing. There are surf schools on the main beach, board shops in town, and places to stay from surf camps to quieter rooms a few streets back.

The town beach gets crowded, especially in the dry season and around the schools. Locals have been surfing here for a long time. Be respectful in the water, wait your turn, and give the inside peak space if you are still finding your feet.

One thing to know: many of the better waves are not in front of the plaza. Schools run daily trips to La Lancha and other nearby breaks. That is often the difference between a busy town-beach session and a much better one.

Getting there

Sayulita is about 50 kilometres (30 miles) north of Puerto Vallarta International Airport (PVR). By car it takes around 45–60 minutes on Highway 200, depending on traffic through Bucerías and the Punta Mita junction.

If you don't drive, colectivos and buses run between Puerto Vallarta, Bucerías and Sayulita. Taxis and ride-hailing apps also work from the airport, though they cost more than the bus.

We'd hire a car if you can, or budget for school shuttles. The town beach is walkable from anywhere in Sayulita, but the best nearby waves — San Pancho and the Punta Mita beaches — are a short drive away. A car makes it easy to chase the best conditions.

Surfing Sayulita by season

The "best" season depends on what you want. Warm, small waves and a buzzing town? Come in winter. Bigger swell and fewer people in the streets? Come in summer. Here's how each season breaks down.

🌸 Spring (March–May)

Best for: improving beginners and anyone who wants dry weather without the Christmas crowds.

Early spring still feels like the dry season: sunny days, smaller swell, and a town that is busy at weekends but calmer than December. The water is at its coolest in March, then warms quickly.

By May the first proper south swells of the year start to arrive. That is when the town beach has a bit more push, and when trips to La Lancha become more worthwhile. May is an underrated month — still mostly dry, with better waves than mid-winter.

Average conditions

  • Water temperature: 24–27°C (75–81°F)
  • Air temperature: 26–32°C (79–90°F)
  • Recommended wetsuit: none; rashguard for sun

Best regions

  • Sayulita town beach for learning and longboarding
  • San Pancho and La Lancha on the bigger days

☀️ Summer (June–August)

Best for: intermediates and advanced surfers who want more size; confident beginners with a school.

Summer is the wet season, and it is when this coast gets its most powerful surf. South and south-west groundswell, plus the occasional tropical-storm pulse, lights up the town beach and the Punta Mita points.

The trade-off is weather. Days are hot and humid, and short, heavy rains are common in the afternoon. The town is quieter than in winter, which we like. Surf in the morning, before the wind and the storms.

The water is at its warmest. You will not need a wetsuit.

Average conditions

  • Water temperature: 28–30°C (82–86°F)
  • Air temperature: 28–33°C (82–91°F)
  • Recommended wetsuit: none; rashguard for sun

Best regions

  • Punta Mita spots (La Lancha, and heavier points on the right day)
  • Sayulita town beach when the swell is not too big
  • San Pancho for a punchier beach break with more space

🍂 Autumn (September–November)

Best for: every level. This is the all-round sweet spot.

If we could only surf Sayulita once a year, we'd come in November. September can still hold some of the year's biggest swell, and the rains have not fully gone. October eases off. By November the dry weather is returning, the water is still tropical, and the winter crowds have not fully arrived.

Early autumn suits confident surfers who don't mind a stormy day. Late autumn suits almost everyone.

Average conditions

  • Water temperature: 27–30°C (81–86°F)
  • Air temperature: 26–32°C (79–90°F)
  • Recommended wetsuit: none; a rashguard on cooler November dawns

Best regions

  • The Punta Mita beaches on leftover south swell
  • Sayulita town beach as the waves ease towards winter
  • San Pancho for a quieter session

❄️ Winter (December–February)

Best for: beginners, longboarders, and anyone who wants sun and smaller waves.

Winter is the dry season, and it is peak holiday time. The streets are busy, the town beach is busy, and the waves are usually smaller and more forgiving. That makes it the safest and friendliest time to learn.

North and north-west swell keeps things consistent even when it is not big. Mornings are cooler, but you still surf in boardshorts. We go out at first light, before the schools and the holiday crowd fill the peak.

Average conditions

  • Water temperature: 23–25°C (73–77°F)
  • Air temperature: 22–29°C (72–84°F)
  • Recommended wetsuit: none; rashguard or thin shorty at dawn

Best regions

  • Sayulita town beach for learning
  • San Pancho when the main beach is too crowded

Is the water cold?

No. If you have surfed in Europe, Sayulita will feel like a warm bath. If you are coming from somewhere tropical, February dawns can feel fresh for the first few minutes, then you forget about it.

The key is sun protection, not insulation. We have never cut a session short here because of the cold.

Regional variation

The differences along this short stretch of coast are small. Shallow sand at the town beach can feel a touch warmer than deeper water off the Punta Mita points. Wind and early starts make more difference than geography. Pack a rashguard and you'll be fine.

The best surf spots in Sayulita

The waves here split neatly by area. The town beach is the sandy, convenient break. A few minutes north, San Pancho is punchier and less crowded. A short drive south, the Punta Mita beaches — especially La Lancha — add quality when you want a change.

Sayulita town beach

This is the wave in front of town, a sandy beach break with peaks that shift with tide and sand. On small days it is soft and perfect for learning. On a decent south swell it gets steeper, faster, and much busier. Most Sayulita surf schools run from here.

The inside is the place for first-timers. The outside peak is where the more experienced surfers sit. Give that peak space if you are still getting to your feet.

The main peak

Right in front of the beach access from the plaza. This is the classic Sayulita wave: a mix of lefts and rights, often best on a smaller, cleaner morning. It is also the most crowded spot in town.

Best for:

  • Beginners on small days
  • Longboarders
  • A quick session without leaving town

The south end

Walk towards the rocks at the southern end of the beach and the crowd thins a little. The wave can be a bit more organised here, with a longer left on the right day. Still sandy, still close to town.

Best for:

  • Improving beginners
  • Anyone escaping the main-peak crowd

Who should visit Sayulita town beach?

  • Beginners and first-timers
  • Families and casual surfers
  • Longboarders after mellow waves
  • Anyone who wants a wave they can walk to

San Pancho

San Pancho (San Francisco) is about ten minutes north of Sayulita. It is a quieter town with a more powerful beach break. The wave has more punch than Sayulita's main beach, and there is more room to spread out.

It is tide-sensitive, and it is not always as consistent as the town beach in Sayulita. When it is on, it is a lovely step up. See surf in San Pancho for listings.

Best for:

  • Intermediates
  • Beginners on smaller days with a school
  • Anyone who wants space away from Sayulita's main peak

Who should visit San Pancho?

  • Surfers who dislike crowds
  • Improvers ready for a punchier beach break
  • Anyone mixing a few nights in a calmer town

La Lancha and the Punta Mita beaches

South of Sayulita, towards Punta Mita, the coast turns up a handful of better-quality waves. La Lancha is the one you will hear about most: a pretty beach with sand and some reef, peaks for several levels, and regular school trips from town. El Anclote is mellower. Punta Burros is the heavier option, and not a place for beginners.

None of these are separate town hubs on Surflist. Treat them as day trips from Sayulita. A hire car or a surf-school shuttle is the usual way to get there.

Best for:

  • Intermediates wanting cleaner peaks than the town beach
  • Advanced surfers on the heavier points
  • Anyone chasing conditions on a south-swell day

Who should visit the Punta Mita beaches?

  • Improvers and intermediates on a school trip
  • Advanced surfers looking for more power
  • Anyone happy to leave town for the morning

Our personal favourite

If we had to choose, we'd split our time between the town beach at first light and La Lancha when the swell is on. Sayulita's main beach is the reason we came: sandy, convenient, and friendly on a small day. La Lancha is the session we talk about afterwards. When the town beach is too crowded, we drive ten minutes to San Pancho. That mix — a walk-to wave plus two nearby options — is what makes this stretch of Nayarit work.

The best beginner surf spots in Sayulita

Sayulita is one of the best places on Mexico's Pacific coast to learn. The town beach is sandy, the water is warm, and there are lots of surf schools. Here are the spots we'd point a new surfer towards.

1. Sayulita town beach (inside)

Best for: first-ever lessons and nervous beginners.

Stay on the inside whitewater. The bottom is sand, the waves are slow, and you are a short walk from town. This is the classic learner's beach. Browse Sayulita surf schools.

Why we recommend it:

  • Sandy bottom, so falls are soft
  • Gentle, rolling waves on small days
  • Plenty of surf schools on the beach
  • Easy to reach from anywhere in town

2. El Anclote (Punta Mita)

Best for: beginners who want a change of scene on a small day.

El Anclote is a mellower beach near Punta Mita, often used when the town beach is messy or too busy. It is a day trip, not a walk from the plaza. Go with a school or someone who knows the access.

Why we recommend it:

  • Softer waves than the heavier Punta Mita points
  • A break from the town-beach crowd
  • Warm, clear water

3. San Pancho on a small day

Best for: beginners who have had a few lessons and want more space.

San Pancho is punchier than Sayulita, so it is not the first beach we'd pick for a complete beginner. On a small, clean day, with a school, it is a lovely place to practise away from the main peak.

Why we recommend it:

  • More room in the line-up
  • A short drive from Sayulita
  • A natural step up once you are standing

What makes a good beginner surf beach?

When we point a new surfer somewhere, we look for a few simple things. A sandy bottom, so there's nothing hard to hit. Gentle, rolling waves that break slowly and give you time to stand up. Warm water, so you can stay out long enough to practise. Enough space so you're not fighting for waves. And a good surf school nearby, because a lesson or two will speed you up far more than going it alone. Sayulita ticks all of these, which is why it's such a fine place to start.

Should beginners surf in winter?

Yes — winter is the easiest time to learn in Sayulita. The waves are usually smaller and softer, and the weather is dry. The surf schools are all running. The one downside is crowds. The town beach gets busy, especially around Christmas and Easter. To get the best of it, surf early in the morning before the beach fills up. November and May are quieter alternatives with water that is still warm.

Sayulita surf trip packing list

🏄 Surf essentials

Surfboard

If you're a beginner, a big soft-top or a longer foam board is your friend — they're stable and easy to catch waves on. Most people hire one for the first few days, which saves flying with a board. Improvers and confident surfers will want a board that suits the conditions: a longboard or mid-length for the town beach, and a shortboard for punchier days at San Pancho or La Lancha.

Wetsuit

You almost certainly don't need one. Pack boardshorts and a long-sleeve rashguard. A thin shorty is optional for the coolest February dawns, and that's it.

Rashguard and sun protection

This matters more than rubber. The sun is strong every month, and reflected glare off the water burns fast. Bring a UV rashguard, a cap for the beach, and reef-safe sunscreen. Reapply.

Surf wax

Bring warm-water wax. It's easy to buy in town too.

Changing poncho or towel

A light towel is enough here. You won't be shivering in a car park the way you would in Europe.

Useful extras

  • Reusable water bottle
  • Dry bag for wet gear
  • Waterproof phone pouch
  • Small first aid kit
  • Reef-safe sunscreen
  • Flip-flops / sandals
  • Insect repellent (wet season)
  • Snorkel and mask for flat days

The best Sayulita trip isn't the one with the biggest waves. It's the one where you surf the town beach at dawn, take a trip down to La Lancha when the swell is on, and slip up to San Pancho when the main peak fills up. Say yes to the early session, then eat tacos in the plaza. Surf the beach that suits the day, not the one with the most people on it — and you'll leave already planning the next visit.

FAQs

Is Sayulita good for beginner surfers?

Yes. The town beach is a sandy beach break with soft, rolling waves on most days, and there are plenty of surf schools. Winter and early spring are the gentlest. Stay off the heavier Punta Mita points until you have more experience.

What is the best time of year to surf in Sayulita?

It depends on your level. Beginners do best from November to April, when the waves are smaller and the weather is dry. Intermediate and advanced surfers should aim for May to October, when south and south-west swell is bigger. Our all-round pick is November: warm water, fewer storms, and the crowds have not fully arrived.

Can you surf in Sayulita all year round?

Yes. There are waves every month. The town beach is consistent, and nearby spots around Punta Mita and San Pancho pick up different swell, so something is nearly always working.

Do I need a wetsuit in Sayulita?

No. The water stays warm all year, from about 23°C (73°F) in late winter to 30°C (86°F) in late summer. Boardshorts and a rashguard are enough. A thin shorty is only useful on the coolest winter dawns.

Is Sayulita or San Pancho better for surfing?

Sayulita is easier for beginners: a sandy town beach, lots of schools, and a short walk from anywhere in town. San Pancho, ten minutes north, is a punchier beach break with more space. Many trips use both.

Is La Lancha good for surfing?

Yes. It is one of the best nearby waves — a pretty beach with peaks for several levels, about 25 minutes south towards Punta Mita. Local schools run regular trips there. It is not a separate town hub on Surflist; treat it as a day trip from Sayulita.

Can you longboard in Sayulita?

Yes. The town beach is a classic longboard wave on small, clean days. Winter and early spring are best. San Pancho and La Lancha also have long, rideable walls when the swell is not too heavy.

Is surfing in Sayulita expensive?

It is mid-range for a Mexican surf town. Lessons, board hire and food are reasonable, especially in the wet season. Accommodation and restaurants cost more from December to April, so travel in May, June or November to save money.

Is Sayulita a good destination for a surf road trip?

It is a great base for a short one. San Pancho is ten minutes north, and the Punta Mita spots are a short drive south. Puerto Vallarta airport is close. A hire car or a school shuttle makes it easy to chase the best conditions.
