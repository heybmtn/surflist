The Ultimate Guide to Surfing in Ericeira (2026): Best Seasons, Surf Spots & Water Temperatures

We keep coming back to Ericeira. It's a small fishing town on Portugal's west coast, and it packs more good waves into a short stretch of shore than almost anywhere in Europe. We've had long, easy sessions on the sandy beaches to the south, and we've watched the heavy reefs to the north from the cliffs, coffee in hand, working up the nerve to paddle out. This guide covers everything we wish we'd known before our first trip — the best seasons, the surf spots by skill level, the water temperatures month by month, and what to pack. Whatever your level, and whatever the swell is doing, there's almost always a wave here that suits you.

Europe's first World Surfing Reserve

Ericeira has just 8 kilometres (5 miles) of coastline. But that short stretch holds more than a dozen quality waves. In 2011 it became the first World Surfing Reserve in Europe, and it's still the only one on the continent.

Seven of its waves are protected as world-class: Pedra Branca, Reef, Ribeira d'Ilhas, Cave, Crazy Left, Coxos and São Lourenço. The reserve status matters in real life. It limits building on the cliffs and keeps the water cleaner, so the waves stay as good as they are.

Waves all year round

You can surf here every month of the year. Each season has its own feel. Spring brings clean, medium swells and fewer people. Summer is small and gentle, which is perfect for learning. Autumn is the sweet spot, with the first big swells and water still warm from summer. Winter is powerful, cold and mostly for experienced surfers.

Where the swell comes from

Ericeira faces west and north-west, straight into the open Atlantic. That's the key to its consistency.

Storms far out in the North Atlantic send swell towards this coast all year. In winter those storms are strong, so the waves get big. In summer they're weaker, so the waves are smaller.

The coast also bends and twists, and the reefs and points all face slightly different ways. So when one spot is too big or too messy, another one is usually working. That's why we rarely get skunked here.

Suits every skill level

This is the thing we love most about Ericeira. The beaches to the south have soft, sandy waves that are great for beginners. The reefs to the north are fast and heavy, and they'll test the best surfers in Europe. In between, there are long, peeling waves for intermediates. Browse surf schools in Ericeira if you want a lesson.

Longboarders are well looked after too. The mellow points and gentle beach days are made for slow, stylish surfing.

Climate and lifestyle

The weather here is mild and easy. Summers are warm and dry. Winters are cooler and a bit wetter, but rarely freezing. Air temperatures sit around 22–25°C (72–77°F) in summer and 11–15°C (52–59°F) in winter.

Between sessions there's plenty to do. The old town has cobbled streets, small squares and some of the best seafood in Portugal. (The name Ericeira comes from ouriço, the Portuguese word for sea urchin.) You can also drive to the huge Mafra Palace, the hills of Sintra, or Lisbon itself in under an hour.

Surf culture

Ericeira lives and breathes surfing. There are surf schools on the beginner beaches, board shops in town, and a strong local scene that has been here for decades. Places to stay range from surf camps to quieter guesthouses.

Every autumn the town hosts a big contest at Ribeira d'Ilhas: the EDP Ericeira Pro, part of the WSL Challenger Series. In 2026 it runs from 6–12 October. It's the only European stop on that tour, and it's free to watch from the cliffs.

One thing to know: the locals are proud of their waves, and the heavier reefs have their own pecking order. Be respectful in the water, wait your turn, and you'll be welcomed.

Getting there

Ericeira is about 45 kilometres (28 miles) from Lisbon Airport. By car it takes around 40–45 minutes on the A8 and A21 motorways. There are small tolls, so keep a little cash handy.

There's no train to Ericeira. If you don't drive, you can take a bus from Campo Grande in Lisbon. The express bus takes about 45 minutes; the slower buses take around 90. Uber and Bolt both work here too.

Porto and Faro airports are options, but both are around a 3-hour drive away, so Lisbon is by far the easiest.

We'd hire a car if you can. The best waves are spread along the coast, not all in the town centre, and a car makes it easy to chase the best conditions. It also puts Peniche, Nazaré and Sintra within an easy day trip.

Surfing Ericeira by season

The "best" season depends on what you want. Warm water and easy waves? Come in summer. Powerful swell and empty line-ups? Come in winter. Here's how each season breaks down.

🌸 Spring (March–May)

Best for: improving beginners and intermediates who want good waves without the summer crowds.

Spring is a quiet, underrated time to surf here. Early spring still gets decent Atlantic swells, and they ease off as summer gets closer.

The water is at its coldest in March, then slowly warms up. The crowds are thin, especially compared with summer. Wind can be hit and miss, so it pays to check the forecast and move around.

By May, the beach breaks are gentle enough for beginners, while the reefs still work on the bigger days.

Average conditions

  • Water temperature: 14–16°C (57–61°F)
  • Air temperature: 15–20°C (59–68°F)
  • Recommended wetsuit: 4/3mm early spring, easing to 3/2mm by May

Best regions

  • The north reefs on clean days
  • The south beach breaks for learning

☀️ Summer (June–August)

Best for: beginners and anyone learning to surf.

Summer has the smallest and most consistent waves of the year. That makes it the safest and friendliest time to learn.

There's a catch. Summer is peak holiday season in Portugal, so the town and the water get busy. And a north wind called the nortada often picks up in the afternoon, which chops the waves up. So we surf in the morning, when the wind is usually calm and the surf is cleanest.

The water is at its warmest, and a light wetsuit is all you need.

Average conditions

  • Water temperature: 17–18°C (63–64°F), reaching 19–20°C (66–68°F) on the warmest days
  • Air temperature: 22–26°C (72–79°F)
  • Recommended wetsuit: 3/2mm

Best regions

  • The south beach breaks (Foz do Lizandro, São Julião)
  • The town beaches for a quick session

🍂 Autumn (September–November)

Best for: every level. This is the all-round sweet spot.

If we could only surf Ericeira once a year, we'd come in autumn. The first proper swells of the season roll in, but the water is still holding its summer warmth. And once the August holidaymakers leave, the line-ups thin right out.

Early autumn is warm and mellow. As you move towards November, the swells get bigger and the water starts to cool.

This is when the reserve reefs really come alive, so it suits confident surfers. But the beaches still work for beginners on the smaller days.

Average conditions

  • Water temperature: 16–18°C (61–64°F)
  • Air temperature: 18–22°C (64–72°F)
  • Recommended wetsuit: 3/2mm, moving to 4/3mm by late November

Best regions

  • The north reefs (Ribeira d'Ilhas, Matadouro)
  • The south beaches on smaller days

❄️ Winter (December–February)

Best for: experienced surfers chasing powerful, world-class waves.

Winter is when Ericeira shows its serious side. The Atlantic storms are strong, so the swells are big and powerful. The famous reefs — Coxos, Cave, Pedra Branca, São Lourenço — can fire on the right day.

This is not a beginner window. The waves are heavy, the wind can be gusty, and the water is cold. But if you can handle it, you'll get some of the best surf in Europe with hardly anyone out.

The water is at its coldest in February, so bring a proper winter wetsuit and boots.

Average conditions

  • Water temperature: 14–16°C (57–61°F)
  • Air temperature: 11–15°C (52–59°F)
  • Recommended wetsuit: 4/3mm with boots; a 5/4mm on the coldest days

Best regions

  • The north reefs on a clean swell
  • The south beaches on smaller days for less-experienced surfers

Ericeira's water temperatures throughout the year

Ericeira sits on the open Atlantic, so the water is never tropical. Cool currents keep it fresh even in summer. The water also lags behind the air by a few weeks, so the warmest sea comes in late summer, not mid-summer.

Is the water cold?

It's cooler than you might hope, but it's manageable. If you've surfed in the UK, Ireland or northern France, Ericeira will feel warmer and easier. If you're coming from somewhere tropical, it'll feel chilly at first.

The key is the right wetsuit. In a good 3/2mm in summer or a 4/3mm in winter, you'll stay warm for a long session. We've never cut a session short here because of the cold.

Regional variation

The differences along this short coast are small, but they exist. The sandy beaches to the south tend to feel a touch warmer, as shallow water heats up faster. Deeper reef spots and windy days can feel a degree or two cooler. Nothing to worry about — just pack for the colder end and you'll be fine.

The best surf spots in Ericeira

The waves here split neatly by area. The south has soft, sandy beach breaks that are great for learning. The town beaches are handy for a quick surf. The north is where the world-class reefs are, and that's where the reserve gets serious.

South Ericeira — the beach breaks

Just south of town you'll find the softest, friendliest waves. The bottom is sand, the waves roll rather than slam, and this is where most surf schools set up. If you're learning or you want a relaxed session, start here. See Ericeira surf schools.

Foz do Lizandro

A wide, sandy beach at a river mouth, about a 20-minute walk or short bike ride from town. The waves are soft and forgiving, and there's plenty of space. This is the spot we send friends who are surfing for the first time. There are cafés right on the beach, so it's an easy place to spend the day.

Best for:

  • Beginners and first-timers
  • Longboarders on a mellow day
  • Families

São Julião

A big, open beach at the southern end of the area. It picks up more swell than anywhere else, so it often has a wave when other spots are flat. That makes it a reliable backup. On small days it's great for beginners; on bigger days it suits intermediates.

Best for:

  • Beginners on small days
  • Intermediates on bigger days
  • Anyone looking for surf when it's flat elsewhere

Praia do Sul

This is the beach right by town, on the Praia da Empa side. It's still sandy, but it's a bit punchier than Foz do Lizandro. A good step up once you've found your feet.

Best for:

  • Improving beginners
  • Intermediates wanting a quick session

Who should visit South Ericeira?

  • Beginners and first-timers
  • Families and casual surfers
  • Longboarders after mellow waves
  • Anyone who wants a wave when the reefs are too big

North Ericeira — the World Surfing Reserve reefs

North of town is where Ericeira earns its reputation. These are reef and point breaks, not sandy beaches. The waves are faster, heavier and more powerful. Some are friendly enough for intermediates; others are strictly for experts.

Ribeira d'Ilhas

The most famous wave in Ericeira, and the most welcoming of the reefs. It's a long, peeling right-hander that works on most tides and holds a range of sizes. On smaller days it's a great spot for intermediates to practise turns. This is also where the pro contest is held each autumn. There's good parking, a café and easy access.

Best for:

  • Intermediates
  • Confident beginners on small, clean days
  • Longboarders

Matadouro

Just north of town, this spot has lefts and rights and plenty of peaks to spread the crowd. It's a solid, fun option for intermediates and a good next step after the beaches.

Best for:

  • Intermediates
  • Surfers wanting variety

Coxos

The heavy hitter. Coxos is a fast, powerful right point that breaks over shallow reef. When it's on, it's one of the best waves in Europe. It's also the wave most likely to hurt a visitor who's out of their depth. Only paddle out here if you have real reef experience and you're honest about your level.

Best for:

  • Advanced surfers only

Pedra Branca

A short, hollow, fast left-hander, often called the best left in the area. It barrels quickly, so you need to be sharp on the take-off. Not for beginners.

Best for:

  • Advanced surfers

Reef, Backdoor and Cave

These are the other serious reefs. Reef is a fast, shallow right that can hurt you. Backdoor next door is a little more forgiving but still packs a punch. Cave is a heavy winter slab that only the brave take on. Great to watch from the cliffs, hard to surf.

Best for:

  • Advanced surfers
  • Everyone else: watching from dry land

São Lourenço

The outer reef. It only breaks on big swells, and it holds size that the other spots can't. When it works, the waves are large and the paddle is long. This is a big-wave spot for experts who know exactly why they're there.

Best for:

  • Big-wave and expert surfers only

Who should visit North Ericeira?

  • Intermediates ready for reef breaks (start at Ribeira d'Ilhas)
  • Advanced surfers chasing powerful waves
  • Anyone who loves watching world-class surf from the cliffs

Our personal favourite

It's hard to beat Ribeira d'Ilhas. It's the one spot that works for almost everyone. As intermediates we've had long, happy sessions there, and it's a joy to watch when the pros are on. The only downside is that everyone knows it, so it gets crowded on the good days. When that happens, we drive five minutes and find something quieter. That's the beauty of a coast with this many waves.

The best beginner surf spots in Ericeira

Ericeira is a brilliant place to learn. The sandy beaches to the south are gentle and safe, there are lots of good surf schools, and the water is warm enough in summer to enjoy long lessons. Here are the spots we'd point a beginner to.

1. Foz do Lizandro

Best for: first-timers and anyone in their first weeks of surfing.

Soft, rolling waves over a sandy bottom, with loads of space and a friendly feel. It's close to town and packed with surf schools. This is the classic Ericeira learning beach. Browse Ericeira surf schools.

Why we recommend it:

  • Sandy bottom, so falls are soft
  • Gentle, rolling waves
  • Plenty of surf schools on the beach
  • Easy to reach from town

2. São Julião

Best for: beginners on smaller days who want a reliable wave.

Because it picks up so much swell, São Julião nearly always has something to surf. On small, clean days it's a lovely beginner beach with room to spread out.

Why we recommend it:

  • Very consistent — often has waves when other spots are flat
  • Big, open beach with lots of space
  • Sandy bottom

3. Praia do Sul

Best for: beginners ready to step up from the gentlest waves.

A little punchier than Foz do Lizandro, and right by town. A good place to practise once you're standing up and want slightly more push.

Why we recommend it:

  • Sandy bottom
  • Close to the town centre
  • A natural step up in difficulty

What makes a good beginner surf beach?

When we judge a beach for first-timers, we look for a few things. We want a sandy bottom, so falls are soft. We want gentle, rolling waves that give you time to stand up and steer. We want consistency, so you're not sitting and waiting. We want space, so you can stay clear of other surfers. And we want good surf schools with local instructors who know the tides. The beaches above tick all those boxes.

Should beginners surf in summer?

Yes — summer is the easiest time to learn here. The waves are smallest and softest, and the water is at its warmest. The trade-off is crowds. Summer is peak season, so the beginner beaches get busy, especially in the middle of the day. Our tip: surf early. The wind is usually calmer in the morning, and the line-up is quieter before the beach fills up.

Ericeira surf trip packing list

🏄 Surf essentials

Surfboard

If you're a beginner, a soft-top or a big foam board is ideal for the south beaches. Most surf schools provide boards, so you may not need your own. Intermediates will want a shortboard or a funboard for the beaches and Ribeira d'Ilhas. If you're chasing the winter reefs, bring a step-up board for bigger, faster waves.

Wetsuit

This is the one thing you must get right. In summer, a 3/2mm is enough. In spring and autumn, pack a 4/3mm for the cooler weeks. In winter, bring a 4/3mm plus boots, and a 5/4mm if you feel the cold.

Surf wax

Bring cool-water wax for most of the year. In the coldest winter weeks, cold-water wax works better. It's easy to buy locally too.

Changing poncho / towel

A changing poncho makes cold car-park changes far easier, especially in winter or when there's wind. We wouldn't travel without one.

Useful extras

  • Reusable water bottle
  • Dry bag for wet wetsuits
  • Waterproof phone pouch
  • Small first aid kit
  • Earplugs (surfer's ear is a real thing on this cool coast)
  • Ding repair kit
  • Flip-flops / sandals
  • Snorkel and mask for flat days

The best trip here isn't the one with the biggest waves. It's the one where you go with the conditions — surf the beaches when the reefs are too big, come early to beat the wind, and take time to enjoy the town, the seafood and the cliffs between sessions. Do that, and you'll leave already planning your next visit.

FAQs

Is Ericeira good for beginner surfers?

Yes, it's one of the best places in Europe to learn. The sandy beaches to the south, like Foz do Lizandro and São Julião, are soft and forgiving, and there are lots of surf schools. Just stay away from the north reefs until you're much more experienced.

What is the best time of year to surf in Ericeira?

For most people, September and October are ideal. The swell is good, the water is still fairly warm, and the summer crowds have gone. Beginners often do best in summer, and advanced surfers should aim for winter.

Can you surf in Ericeira all year round?

Yes. There are waves every month. The coast catches swell in all seasons, and its many spots face different ways, so something is nearly always working.

Do I need a wetsuit in Ericeira?

Yes, all year. The water ranges from about 14°C (57°F) in winter to 18°C (64°F) in late summer. A 3/2mm suits summer, while a 4/3mm (plus boots) is best in winter.

Is South or North Ericeira better for surfing?

It depends on your level. The south has gentle beach breaks that are great for beginners and relaxed sessions. The north has the world-class reefs for intermediates and experts. Many trips mix both.

Is Ribeira d'Ilhas good for surfing?

Very. It's the most famous and most welcoming of Ericeira's reefs — a long right-hander that works for intermediates and even confident beginners on small days. It's also where the pro contest is held each autumn.

Can you longboard in Ericeira?

Yes. The mellow beach breaks and the long, peeling wave at Ribeira d'Ilhas are made for longboarding. Summer and clean autumn days are best.

Is surfing in Ericeira expensive?

No, it's good value. Board and wetsuit hire, lessons and food are all reasonable, especially outside summer. Car hire and accommodation cost more in July and August, so travel in spring or autumn to save money.

Is Ericeira a good destination for a surf road trip?

It's a great base for one. Peniche and Baleal are about 50 minutes north, Nazaré is close, and Lisbon is a short drive south. With a hire car, you can chase the best waves along a big stretch of coast.
